Technical due diligence in Berlin and Brandenburg.

Inspection of the property as far as accessible, review of the documents and a report on condition, defects and costs. For investors, private buyers and lenders.

Who it is for

International investors

Apartment buildings, mixed-use buildings and small portfolios. The report contains a technical risk picture and a capex plan that a bank and a lawyer can work with.

Private buyers

A house or a flat in Berlin. For a single flat or house there is the pre-purchase inspection with a written condition report.

Lenders and asset managers

Condition and maintenance backlog as a basis for financing decisions, refurbishment budgets and hold/sell strategies.

Scope of the inspection

  • Structure and envelope – load-bearing walls, ceilings, cracks, settlement, façade, balconies
  • Roof and drainage – covering, flashings, gutters, attic, signs of leaks
  • Basement, damp and mould – rising damp, waterproofing, ventilation, salt efflorescence
  • Building services – heating, electrics, water and waste pipes, lifts, age and remaining life
  • Fire safety and escape routes – visual check of obvious risks in multi-unit buildings
  • Energy and refurbishment duties – energy certificate, insulation, obligations under the German Buildings Energy Act (GEG)
  • Documents – building permits, register of public easements (Baulastenverzeichnis), land register section II, maintenance records, protocols of the owners' association
  • Maintenance backlog – what has been postponed, what it costs and when it becomes urgent

What you receive

An executive summary with red flags, findings per building part with photos, defects classified by urgency (immediate · 1–3 years · 4–10 years), cost ranges for repair and refurbishment, a 10-year capex plan and a document checklist with gaps. In English; German on request.

Limits of the service

The office does not broker, does not build and is not paid by the seller. Legal and tax advice are not part of the service. Opening of building parts and laboratory tests are arranged separately if a finding requires them.

How it works

  1. Enquiry and documents. Object, questions and timeline are clarified by email. The list of documents to request from the seller is provided.
  2. Site inspection. Access is coordinated with seller, agent or property manager; all accessible areas are inspected, with the client or on the client's behalf. Usually within five working days.
  3. Red-flag summary. Within 48 hours: whether anything speaks against the purchase or should change the price.
  4. Report and debrief. Full report within seven to ten working days, followed by a call on findings, costs and next steps.

Frequently asked questions

Can the inspection take place if the buyer is not in Berlin?

Yes. Access is coordinated with the seller, agent or property manager, the inspection takes place on site and the findings are discussed by video call. The report is written in English; German on request.

How quickly is a report delivered?

An inspection date within Berlin is usually available within five working days. For due diligence a red-flag summary follows within 48 hours of the inspection and the full report within seven to ten working days.

What if the seller does not provide documents?

The report states which documents are missing and what that means. Some can be obtained from the authorities, for example the register of public easements (Baulastenverzeichnis). Missing documents are listed as a risk.

Is the due diligence also a valuation?

No. The report assesses condition, risks and costs. A first value range is available from the value calculator; a formal market valuation is a separate task.

Which areas are covered?

Berlin, with travel within Berlin included; the surrounding Brandenburg region by arrangement.
